Ford Recalls 223,472 F-150s Because Their Fuel Tanks Can Come Loose

SO
Shedrack Olisa
September 17, 2026 · 5 min read

Ford is recalling 223,472 F-150 pickups because the straps that hold the fuel tank in place may not have been locked into the frame when the trucks were built. On an affected truck, the tank can work loose, leak fuel, and in the worst case drop onto the road while the truck is moving.

The headline number is large, but the recall filing Ford submitted to federal regulators tells a more specific story than most coverage has, and it changes what owners should actually do.

The Fault

Each F-150 has front fuel tank straps with T-shaped ends that slot into the frame rail. According to Ford's Part 573 safety recall report, filed with NHTSA on September 9, those T-slots were in some cases not properly inserted during assembly.

Ford's plants use camera systems to confirm the straps are seated correctly. The filing says those vision systems were "not in continuous use" at F-150 assembly plants at various points between January 4, 2023 and August 27, 2026. Trucks that passed through the line during those gaps are the ones in question.

The risks listed in the filing are fuel leaks, which raise the chance of fire or of the engine stalling as fuel is lost, and a detached tank becoming a hazard for traffic behind. Ford also states there is no warning a driver would see before it happens.

Act on a fuel smell: Because there is no dashboard warning, a smell of fuel around the truck, visible drips underneath, or a knock from below the cab over bumps should be treated as a reason to stop driving and call a dealer, not something to monitor.

Why Your Older F-150 Is Probably Not Included

This is the detail worth understanding. The recall does not cover every F-150 built since 2023. Ford's filing limits it to trucks from that production window that were either unsold, or had been in service for less than three months, as of August 27, 2026.

Ford explains why. Its field data shows the condition "will manifest very early in the vehicle's lifetime." In plain terms, a strap that was never seated properly tends to show itself quickly. A 2023 F-150 that has covered two years of normal driving without trouble has, in Ford's judgment, already proved its straps are holding.

That is why the recall covers model years 2023 through 2027 but is dominated in practice by recently delivered and dealer-stock trucks. It is also why the VINs are not in sequence, so the only reliable way to know is to check.

How Ford Found It

The filing's chronology is short. The issue reached Ford's Critical Concern Review Group on July 23, 2026. Through July and August, the group reviewed global warranty data and traced unsecured tanks to trucks built while the vision systems were not running continuously. As of August 27, Ford knew of 16 warranty claims worldwide. Its Field Review Committee approved the recall on September 1.

Ford says it is not aware of any crashes or injuries linked to the condition.

What Owners Should Do

  • Check your VIN now. VINs became searchable on September 14 through the NHTSA recall lookup, or you can call Ford on 1-866-436-7332.
  • Watch for the letter. Owner notifications are scheduled to be mailed between September 21 and September 28. Ford's recall number is 26S69; NHTSA's is 26V578.
  • Book the inspection. Dealers will check the front fuel tank straps and refit or replace them as needed, free of charge. The replacement part is the same strap, correctly secured.
  • Buying new? Unsold stock is part of this recall, so ask the dealer to confirm the inspection has been done before you take delivery.

Ford has not issued a do-not-drive or park-outside advisory for this campaign.

The Wider Picture

The F-150 is the best-selling vehicle line in the United States, so even a fault Ford estimates affects around one truck in a hundred touches a lot of driveways. The underlying lesson is about process rather than parts: the strap itself is not defective, and the fix is to fit the same component properly. What failed was the check meant to catch a mistake on the line.

If you drive a recently delivered F-150, the check takes two minutes. Do it this week.
